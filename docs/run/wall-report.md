## How it is put together

**The household device.** The wall must never hold a person's session (brief, and 0160). The least new machinery that keeps every access rule is an ordinary *user* that is an ordinary *member* of the family team: the device user "Wall". It reads the family space under exactly the row policies any member has, and it can reach nothing personal because it is nobody's self. It holds one scoped API token (`routes: ["wall"]`), the kind the phone already holds for capture, kept in an httpOnly cookie because the wall is a page, not a script; the middleware reads that cookie on `/api/wall/*` only (step 3c), so even a wall cookie sent elsewhere opens nothing, and the e2e spec proves it against the family, tickets, settings and finance routes. Making the device user goes through the doors a person would go through: an invite row (the invite-only trigger refuses anything else), the Auth admin API to create the account, and `accept_invite` as that user to join the team. No policy was loosened and no trigger bypassed; the service role does only the bookkeeping in two new platform tables, `wall_devices` and `wall_pairings`, which have no policy for `authenticated` at all. Pairing is an eight-digit code shown once in Settings, good for ten minutes, redeemed once by the wall (rate-limited by address); the token is kept only until it is redeemed. Revoking a wall revokes its token, spends its codes and removes the device from the team.

**Quick unlock.** A PIN of four to eight digits a person sets in Settings → Security, hashed (PBKDF2-SHA-256, a salt a row) in `quick_unlocks`, an owner-only table in the 0111 shape in the person's own space. On the wall a tap on a person opens the keypad; the server checks the PIN with the service role (the wall has no session for the person at that moment), only for a member of the wall's team, throttled per wall and per person, and answers a wrong PIN and a missing PIN the same way. On success it signs a ten-minute **note** (HMAC over person, device and expiry, derived from the JWT secret with a label so a note is never a JWT and a JWT never a note) into a second httpOnly cookie scoped to `/api/wall`. The person routes verify the note and then act for the person with the minted-JWT client every system route already uses, within those routes alone: their Home contract, done / start / not today on their items, the morning read aloud, a capture. The screen never sees a token for the person, and the server forgets the person at ten minutes whatever the screen does; the screen closes the view itself after two quiet minutes and clears the cookie.

**What the board shows and why.** The §11 glance says "next things" per person. On the family board that would put a person's ticket titles on a shared screen with no unlock, which the same section forbids a line later; the board therefore shows each person's day as numbers and the week as fill (read server-side as that person, read-only: `peekHome` makes no plan), and the titles wait for the unlock. Phil can move titles onto the board in a line if he decides the household may see them.

**Speech.** The interruption ledger already writes a `wall` channel row alongside a nudge or the morning pick, with the spoken wording (`lib/nudges/interrupt.ts`, lane F's budget rules). The wall is the first reader of those rows: `thingsToSay` hands over the unspoken ones from the last ten minutes for the team's people and marks them acted as it does, so a line is said once and the "nudge response" measure sees it. Family appointment reminders now carry a spoken line as well. Finance kinds are filtered out at that point even though nothing writes them to the wall channel today.

**No new dependencies.** Speech is `speechSynthesis`, recording is `MediaRecorder`, the chime is the Home one, the backdrop and specimens are the Home components, weather is the existing open-meteo call.

## Found on the way

- **Survey.** Beacon (MIT; touch-first, anti-burn-in screensaver), HomeOS (a kitchen touchscreen family board), Magic Frame (photo rotation when idle), MagicMirror² (regions and modules on a Raspberry Pi), and the commercial DAKboard, Skylight and Mango for what people pay for. Taken: a regions layout rather than a dashboard of cards, idle photo rotation and a figure that moves against burn-in, touch-first controls sized for a finger from standing, and a guest or screensaver mode as a first-class state. Not taken: module marketplaces, weather-first layouts, anything that needs a second server.
- **`peekHome`.** Loading Home made today's plan if there was none (`ensurePlan`), which is right for the person and wrong for a screen looking in. `lib/home/data.ts` now has `peekHome` (no plan, null) beside `loadHome`; nothing else changed there.
- **The capture-audio route** is now the HTTP shape of `captureRecording` in `lib/capture/receiveAudio.ts`; behaviour unchanged, so the wall and the phone share one path.
- **Local pairing is one address.** Every run on the PC is the same client address, so the pairing bucket (five tries, one a minute) filled across runs until the spec reset it; on the wall itself this is the intended behaviour.
- **The tick-rate lint** (React compiler rules) refused `Date.now()` in a ref initialiser and setState through an async call at the top of an effect; both rewritten, nothing disabled.
- `graphify` is not installed on this PC; the project-side copies of the state documents were not touched (a lane does not write them).

## If the frame cannot run a browser (MYC-182)

The route needs a current browser with touch, speech synthesis and a microphone. A locked photo frame (Aura, Nixplay, Pix-Star and most "digital photo frame" firmware) has none of these; a frame that is really an Android tablet in a frame (Skylight-type, many no-name A3 frames) usually does, through a kiosk browser. Prices are UK street prices seen in 2026 and move; none of this is bought by the run.

| B. Android tablet behind the frame's glass or on its own mount | Fire HD 10 (speakers, mic, touch, 10"), Fully Kiosk, a wall mount | £100–200 | Smallest spend for a full wall; 10" is well short of A3. Needs a Google-free kiosk, which Fully Kiosk is. |

## Phil's live check (a new chunk of MYC-193, 15 minutes)

1. Settings → Security → Quick unlock: set a PIN. Ask Kirsty to set hers on her phone (same card).
2. Settings → Security → Walls: Team "Family", name "Kitchen", **Add wall**. An eight-digit code appears; it is shown once and lasts ten minutes.
3. On the frame (or the phone for now), open `/wall` in the browser and type the code. The family board appears: both names, the countdown, the week's appointments, weather and bins.
4. Tap your name, type your PIN. Your day appears with the next thing large. Tap **Done** on something: the chime. Tap **Read my morning**: the voice. Tap **Tap to talk**, say something, tap again: it is in Captures within a minute.
5. Leave it. After two minutes it is back on the family board on its own; after three more it is the ambient plate; a touch brings the board back.
6. Tap **Guest**. Only the clock, the weather and the pictures; hold the faint mark top-right for two seconds to come back.
7. Settings → Security → Walls → **Revoke** when you are done with the phone, and pair the real frame with a new wall.
8. If the frame has no browser or no way to a kiosk app, pick a line from the table above and say which.
