## 3. What the research found

### 3.1 Paving joints (garden.05)

- Polymeric (resin-bonded) jointing products differ in their minimums: one maker gives a continuous minimum width of 3 mm and 30 mm depth for best performance (25 mm only when the paving is 25 mm thick); EASYJoint says not on joints narrower than 3 mm [1]; Bostik Wide Jointing Compound needs at least 5 mm wide and 25 mm deep [2]; Pavetuf is sold for 3 to 20 mm wide with a 20 mm minimum depth [3]; Marshalls list a 20 mm minimum depth for some products [4]. So "3 mm by 30 mm" is a common target, not a universal rule: the ticket measures, then reads the chosen bag's data sheet.
- Pressure washing lifts jointing sand, which is why garden.05 follows garden.04.

### 3.2 Lawn renovation in South Yorkshire (garden.09)

- **Timing**: UK lawn guides put scarifying, aerating and overseeding in mid-spring, typically April, as the soil warms; March is for clearing debris and moss and a first high cut; May for feeding [5][6]. For overseeding, wait for soil consistently at about 10 C (one source), 8 C at 5 cm (another), with the RHS giving at least 7 C for germination [5][7]. Seed in cold wet soil can rot [5].
- **Order**: scarify, then aerate, then overseed, then top-dress [6]. Do not scarify wet or frosty turf [6]. Keep the seed bed moist until germination, often 7 to 14 days (one source; UNVERIFIED as a general figure).
- **South Yorkshire**: a UK frost-date map gives average last frosts from mid-March on the south-west coast to late April across the Midlands and East Anglia [8]; no Doncaster figure was found (UNVERIFIED). 17 April 2027 is a reasonable target, but the ticket lets the soil thermometer decide the seeding day.
- **Scarifier**: not in the owned tool list; a spring-tine rake works on a small lawn, otherwise hire or buy (to be priced).

### 3.3 Hedge trimming and nesting birds (garden.03)

- Under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 it is an offence, with exceptions, to intentionally take, damage or destroy the nest of any wild bird while it is in use or being built; cutting a hedge is not itself illegal [9]. The RSPB gives the main nesting season as March to September and recommends cutting outside it where possible [10]. **13 March 2027 falls at the start of the nesting season.** The ticket keeps the plan's date and adds a nest check first, leaving any section with an active nest.
