## How it is put together

- **Owner-only, by policy shape, not by filter.** Every gifts table's four policies compare the row's space to the reader's personal space, so a member of a shared space sees zero rows, the people route's gifts section shows zero, the count on the People list is zero, and a gift aimed at a person or an occasion the caller cannot see is refused with *No such person* / *No such occasion* rather than written. The isolation assertion in the spec (§11) is written out in the Tess spec as its own checks.
- **The pure parts have no I/O**: the extraction, the occasions plan (roll-forward, 29 February, the double run), the reminder decision (exactly 30 days out fires once, then the next lead day, then two days out when nothing is bought), the money totals with nulls, the purchase mapping. The engine and the routes are thin over them.
- **Nothing is auto-created from a capture.** A recipient is matched by alias or left as a name on the gift (`for_label`); an occasion is matched or left empty; the person picker on the review card shows the matches. The capture pipeline's existing review flow carries the gift the way it carries a quote, including the keep-existing choice for a near-duplicate.
- **The crons are on the tick** (London hours 8–9, once an hour), not in `vercel.json`: the Hobby cron gate stands. The schedule test's 21:00 list is unchanged.
- **The morning nudge never names anyone** (§12 flag 3): the text is counts and a horizon, the detail waits behind the page. One a day, inside the three.
- **The import script talks to the public API as Phil**, so the tickets carry keys, land in Backlog through the same path the UI uses, and never touch the daily proposal (undated Backlog is never drawn from).

## Design calls to review (the spec is silent or left it open)

1. The classifier kind is `gift_idea` everywhere (the model prompt, the regex fallback, the registry's typed form, the review card's kind list). A gift wins over a quote unless the word *quote* appears.
2. 29 February birthdays fall on 28 February in a common year.
3. The occasions engine also runs on demand from the page (*Refresh occasions*, a POST as the signed-in user) so a new date of birth shows up without waiting for the morning; the cron still runs for everyone.
4. Both gift crons sit on the tick at London 8–9, once an hour: the occasions first, the reminders after, before the finance nudge at 9.
5. The reminder task goes to **Next** with a deadline a week before the day; the occasion remembers its task id so a second morning makes nothing. Marking any gift for that occasion bought or given completes the task.
6. The nudge is one a day, counts only, through the interruption budget; nothing when nothing is due; no nudge for an occasion with a gift given.
7. A named person wins over a free-text name on a gift; setting one clears the other.
8. Deleting an occasion leaves its gifts with their date and no occasion (the FK sets null); deleting a gift is allowed from the sheet but the sheet's copy says reject is the record-keeping move.
9. Rejected gifts are out of every list and export by default and in with `include_rejected=1` or the status chip; the sheet can bring one back to ideas.
10. The import keeps the existing script's choices: project status *paused* for anything the harvest calls paused or abandoned, *active* for alive; tickets as tasks in Backlog with points S/M/L → 1/3/8 and tags `harvest` plus the item's type; descriptions carry the detail, the source file and whether it was inferred.
